## Lynds — Academy Lesson 1: Cornering

The most useful of the four by a distance, because it does not give a list of tips. It gives a
**taxonomy of six corner types**, each with its own correct technique. Coach has exactly one
corner type.

| Corner type | Stance | Fore/aft | Counter-lean | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Flat** (Forest, Club, 90° SX turns) | Sit | Forward, middle-to-front of seat | About halfway | The one place counter-lean still earns its keep. Full counter-lean stands the bike up and it runs straight |
| **Smooth rut** | Either; sit for slow, stand for fast | Central or forward | **No** | Ruts lock the bike, so throttle control matters less. Lean angle must be set *before* the rut |
| **Hooked rut** (tightens at exit) | **Stand** | Forward | **No** | Sitting means the bike holds its old angle at the hook and goes over the rut. Standing absorbs the angle change |
| **Rough rut** (pro tracks) | **Stand** | Forward, after the braking bumps | **No** | Standing lets the suspension work. Counter-leaning stands the bike up at every bump |
| **Whooped sand turn** | **Stand** | Lean back off each roller, then forward just before the rear leaves the ground | **No** | Manual every bump, keep the front light. Timing is everything and speed-dependent |
| **Smooth SX berm** | Sit | Forward | Little to none | Roll on early and progressively, before halfway. Stand the bike upright before the next jump face |

Other points from the lesson:

- **Enter the rut at its start, not a third of the way through**, with the bike already near the
  rut's lean angle. Joining late means cranking the stick hard, the suspension compresses, and
  the rebound sends you straight over the rut.
- **Sequence the inputs, don't stack them.** Enter stood up and leaning back while braking, then
  transition to sitting and leaning forward, then ease into counter-lean. Leaning forward while
  still braking slides the rear; counter-leaning while braking stops the bike turning in.
- **Leaning forward saves front-end tucks** — the weight forces the bars back straight.
- Open flat corners up F1-style, out-apex-out.
- Get used to standing before the ruts come in, rather than switching style mid-race.

## Lynds — Noob to Pro (5000 hours)

Half of this is outside anything Coach can see, and that is worth knowing as much as the rest.

**Inside the telemetry loop:**

- **Stand up and lean forward, body central** is now correct in ~90% of corners. The old meta
  (sit, lean back, counter-lean everywhere) is dead after the physics updates. Sit and
  counter-lean only on flat corners.
- **Hop and double the bumps rather than ploughing through them.** Anything you can double, do.
  Go wide, miss most of the braking bumps, double the last couple into the corner and land on
  the power. Framed as more *reliable*, not just faster.
- **Open the corner up, F1 style** — repeated near-verbatim from the earlier video, including
  the claim that +0.1 mph average is worth about a second a lap.
- **Be comfortable with the front wheel off the ground.** A front wheel on the ground is drag;
  manualing rollers beats hitting the front on each one; several SX tracks need the front picked
  up before a rhythm.
- **Let the bike straighten itself.** If the front washes or the rear swaps, stop turning or
  come off the gas rather than fighting it.

**Outside it:**

- Controller settings (direct lean, gain, linearity) are personal — three top-1% riders quoted
  running direct lean of 40, 80 and 100.
- Bike choice is personal; "meta bike" talk is mostly people wanting to skip their own testing.
- A consistent sound mod as an audio gearing cue (bogging → downshift, revving out → upshift).
- Suspension brand is taste, and changes track to track.
- **Lower graphics settings are a competitive advantage** — low-quality track versions and
  shadows off make bumps, ruts and jump sweet spots far easier to read.
- Bind look-back and look-to-the-side; concede a position rather than get taken out.
- Time and talent are the real limits; ~2,000 hours before a pro podium is realistic.

## Aiden — Top 10 Tips (4000 hours)

- **Braking bumps: lean back and use the throttle to keep the bike level.** Presented as the
  single biggest one, and explicitly *not* setup-dependent.
- **Use a higher gear in braking bumps.** First gear's engine braking is too strong, the bike
  sits revving and the rear comes up. Third is far more controllable.
- **Consistency before speed**, with an actual training protocol: a 10–15 minute timer, and the
  only goal is not crashing. Speed comes later.
- **Then practise one-lap speed** deliberately — reset, drop fuel, keep taking fresh laps.
- **Lean forward on flat corners**; not doing so squats the rear and it slides.
- **Don't counter-lean in a rut that holds you** — it leans the bike too far, catches the inside
  rut and tucks the front.
- **Don't churn your setup or your bike.** Muscle memory needs things to stay still.
- **Learn a scrub properly**: lean the bike over on the lip, lean forward, stay low. Everyone
  does it differently.
- **Lean back over braking bumps, forward in corners and on exits.**
- **Ride harder tracks**; after ~10 hours the stock tracks are teaching you a track, not a game.

## WALK3R — Beginner Tips

Short, and aimed lower. (The title says six; five are given, and the numbering repeats.)

- **Be smooth.** Blipping the throttle is the beginner tell.
- **Counter-lean only when it's needed** — flat corners yes, a deep rut no.
- **Slow down to be consistent.** Being a second or two off your limit and upright beats the
  alternative.
- **Don't scrub or whip until you're good enough.** Crashing costs more than the scrub gains.
- **Brakes barely work on braking bumps and downhills** — engine brake instead.
- **Ride harder tracks** rather than farming the stock ones.
